Two results from Energy Terminal's Emerging Leaders Program, spring 2026 cohort.

**Case competition, winning team**

The case asked what a power plant is worth to the Alberta and Saskatchewan systems if it can
interconnect to either grid. We built a mixed-integer linear program that dispatches the plant
across both markets on public Alberta market data.

- Switching between markets adds roughly 50% revenue, but only for low-marginal-cost units that run most of the time.
- Adding two-hour battery storage sized at 25% of plant capacity recovers the switching value that higher-cost units lose, because the battery covers the 30-minute waiting period between market switches. At a $50/MWh marginal cost, interconnection value rose 237% in our model.

Group D: Jesse Angrist, Yiannis Lempidakis, Martina Bulgarelli and Nikita Pande.

**Deep Decarb challenge, top presentation**

Using MIT's En-Roads simulator, our team built a three-pillar decarbonization strategy across
clean energy, agriculture and land use, and carbon management, and defended the policy levers
behind each one. The scenario reaches a 1.9 °C pathway, cutting net emissions from about
60 Gt CO2e to near zero by 2100 through fossil taxation, renewables and electrification subsidies,
agricultural and land-use cuts, and scaled carbon removal.

Team 5, Rays of Sunshine: Nikita Pande, Yiannis Lempidakis, Lance Zhang and Veronica Guo.
